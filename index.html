Learning route and prerequisites

This independent reading edition follows nine CS230 Autumn 2025 recordings. Recording lecture numbers are 1–6 and 8–10; book chapters are consecutive 1–9. The playlist has no recording Lecture 7. Stanford's recordings establish the topic sequence, while the mathematical developments, programs and worked exercises are independent teaching additions.

Assumed background is undergraduate linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability and basic Python. A matrix represents a linear map; derivatives describe local changes; conditional probability changes the population being measured. The chapters introduce deep-learning-specific terms locally. No training service, GPU or model API is required to run their finite numerical examples.

Read chapters 1–3 first to distinguish a model, its training objective and its deployment decision. Chapter 1 develops prediction risk and scaling rather than treating training loss as a guarantee. Chapter 2 changes the supervision signal through labels, representation learning and weak supervision. Chapter 3 follows an operational recognition system through data, evaluation, thresholds and deployment. Keep the evaluation population explicit: a number from the training population need not describe a deployed decision.

Chapter 4 studies how small perturbations and generative objectives alter that picture. Its guarantees apply to stated threat models or objectives, and its numerical constructions do not certify deployed robustness. Chapter 5 changes the learning signal from a correct answer to consequences over time, then connects preference learning to language-model training. A return, a learned reward and human satisfaction are different quantities.

Chapter 6 turns those distinctions into project decisions: sampling, detector base rates, leakage, development iteration and error analysis. Chapter 7 extends the decision loop to prompts, retrieval and agents. Retrieval evidence, tool permissions and task evaluation remain separate concerns; adding context or additional agents does not by itself establish reliability.

Chapter 8 preserves the speakers' career and project judgments as attributed advice. Its probability and cycle-time models are original illustrations with explicit assumptions. Chapter 9 returns to an already trained model and asks what its signals establish: local gradients, path attributions, activation maps, backward constructions, attention, telemetry and evaluation each inspect a different object. A diagnostic signal narrows a question; controlled comparisons are needed to distinguish causes.

How to use the edition

Each chapter starts with a consequential case, develops the relevant objects and assumptions, then supplies executable fixtures and worked exercises. Timestamp links locate the corresponding source material. Expand a solution only after attempting the problem; the reasoning is part of the chapter, so it is available offline and included when printing.

The source map separates lecture material, research extensions and original teaching constructions section by section. Primary references state what each source supports. Recording transcripts are local machine recognition, not human-edited publisher captions; their timing and technical-word uncertainties are retained in provenance metadata. A checked hash identifies bytes, not transcription accuracy.

The numerical laboratory makes two assumptions visible: event prevalence changes detector precision, and dependence changes the benefit of repeated attempts. Its formulas and illustrative expected counts remain readable without JavaScript. Chapter programs implement their own mathematical models and assertions; successful fixtures establish those finite checks rather than benchmark reproduction.

Use the table of contents for a long chapter, larger text for reading, and the theme control for contrast preference. The bundled equation renderer supports offline use. For a local HTTP server, run python -m http.server 8000 --bind 127.0.0.1 in the book directory and open http://127.0.0.1:8000/ yourself. Opening index.html directly also supports the static reader. The server is optional and binds only to this machine.
