Primary references

  • Szegedy et al. 2013, Intriguing properties of neural networks

    Imperceptible perturbations and transfer (pp. 1-2); penalty formulation of targeted attacks (p. 5).

  • Goodfellow, Shlens, Szegedy 2014, Explaining and Harnessing Adversarial Examples

    Linearity explanation, FGSM, adversarial training weights and effect (pp. 1-7).

  • Kurakin, Goodfellow, Bengio 2016, Adversarial examples in the physical world

    Printed adversarial images remain misclassified through a phone camera (pp. 1, 3).

  • Thys, Van Ranst, Goedeme 2019, Fooling automated surveillance cameras

    Three-term patch loss against YOLOv2; intra-class variety (pp. 1, 3).

  • Papernot et al. 2016, Practical Black-Box Attacks against Machine Learning

    Substitute-model black-box attack, 84.24% on MetaMind (p. 1).

  • Madry et al. 2017, Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks

    Saddle-point objective and PGD (pp. 2-4).

  • Kannan, Kurakin, Goodfellow 2018, Adversarial Logit Pairing

    Logit pairing as a distinct defense (p. 1).

  • Lu, Issaranon, Forsyth 2017, SafetyNet

    Detector-based rejection (p. 1).

  • Athalye, Carlini, Wagner 2018, Obfuscated Gradients Give a False Sense of Security

    7 of 9 ICLR 2018 defenses rely on obfuscated gradients; 6 circumvented completely, 1 partially (p. 1).

  • Wiyatno et al. 2019, Adversarial Examples in Modern Machine Learning: A Review

    Survey cited on slides 15 and 17; ALP listed as circumventable by many-iteration PGD (p. 53, survey summary).

  • Gu, Dolan-Gavitt, Garg 2017, BadNets

    Backdoored networks and street-sign backdoor (pp. 1-2).

  • Perez, Ribeiro 2022, Ignore Previous Prompt

    Goal hijacking and prompt leaking (p. 1).

  • Greshake et al. 2023, Indirect Prompt Injection

    Indirect prompt injection through retrieved data (p. 1).

  • Liu et al. 2023, Prompt Injection attack against LLM-integrated Applications

    31 of 36 applications susceptible (p. 14).

  • Rossi et al. 2024, An Early Categorization of Prompt Injection Attacks

    Direct versus indirect injection; hot-wiring example (p. 2).

  • Bai et al. 2022, Constitutional AI

    Harmlessness training without human harm labels; date 15 Dec 2022 (p. 1).

  • Carlini et al. 2023, Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models

    Diffusion models memorise and emit training images (p. 1).

  • Goodfellow et al. 2014, Generative Adversarial Nets

    Value function, non-saturating cost, k-step schedule, optimal discriminator, JS identity (pp. 3-5).

  • Goodfellow 2016, NIPS 2016 Tutorial: Generative Adversarial Networks

    Zero-sum definition, non-convex caveat, cost-response figure, mode collapse (pp. 22-35).

  • Radford, Metz, Chintala 2015, DCGAN

    Vector arithmetic on face codes (p. 10, Figure 7).

  • Lucic et al. 2017, Are GANs Created Equal?

    Similar scores with enough tuning (p. 1).

  • Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015, Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics

    Origin of diffusion probabilistic models (p. 1).

  • Ho, Jain, Abbeel 2020, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models

    Forward process, marginal, L_simple, Algorithms 1-2, schedule (pp. 2-5).

  • Song et al. 2021, Score-Based Generative Modeling through SDEs

    Variance-exploding versus variance-preserving processes (p. 5).

  • Dhariwal, Nichol 2021, Diffusion Models Beat GANs on Image Synthesis

    Guidance trades diversity for fidelity; more modes and recall; Figure 6 FIDs (pp. 1, 10, 11).

  • Rombach et al. 2021 (CVPR 2022), High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models

    Latent autoencoder, downsampling factor, conditioning by concatenation or cross-attention (pp. 1-5).
